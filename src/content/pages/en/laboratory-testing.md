    body: |-
      Laboratory tests examine a sample of your body tissues, blood or urine. Our technician or doctor inspect the test samples to see if your results fall within the normal range.     heading: What are Prenatal Tests?
    body: |-
      Prenatal testing, including diagnostic tests and screening , can supply significant data about your baby's health.

      Pregnancy is a time of special expectation— and, sometimes, agitation. You might worry that your baby will have health problems. While most babies are born healthy, it's important to understand your options for obtaining details about your baby's health.
    tone: default
  - type: contentSection
    heading: Types of prenatal testing
    body: |-
      The two main types of prenatal testing are:

      Screening tests. Prenatal screening tests can recognize whether your baby is more or less likely to have certain birth weaknesses, many of which are genetic disorders. These tests include prenatal cell-free DNA screening, blood tests and a particular type of ultrasound. Prenatal screening tests are generally offered during the first or second trimester. Screening tests can't make a decisive diagnosis. If results indicate an increased risk for a genetic disorder, your health care provider will discuss your alternatives for a diagnostic test to confirm the diagnosis.

      Diagnostic tests. If a screening test indicates a possible problem — or your family history, your age or medical history puts you at increased risk of having a baby with a genetic problem — you might consider an invasive prenatal diagnostic test. A diagnostic test is the only way to be sure of a diagnosis. Some diagnostic tests, such as chorionic villus sampling and amniocentesis, carry a slight risk of miscarriage.

    heading: What Is Ambulatory Blood Pressure Monitoring?
    body: Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) is when your blood pressure is measured while you are moving around, living your normal daily life. It is measured for up to 24 hours. A small digital blood pressure monitor is attached to a belt around your waist and connected to a cuff around your upper arm. It is small enough not to affect your normal daily life and you can even sleep with it on.
    tone: subtle
  - type: contentSection
    heading: Why do I need this research?
    body: |-
      There are several reasons why we might need this:

      - To establish a diagnosis of high blood pressure (hypertension)
      - To identify patients who have higher blood pressure readings when in the clinic (known as the "white coat effect")
      - To help decide if blood pressure medication is needed
      - To help decide if any changes to your medication are needed.
      - To further investigate people whose blood pressure is difficult to control
      - To see how well a patient's blood pressure medications control blood pressure throughout the day
      - To see what happens to a patient's blood pressure overnight

      How is it performed?

      This type of blood pressure measurement is similar to your regular blood pressure measurement: An electronic monitor takes your blood pressure by inflating a cuff around your upper arm, then slowly releasing the pressure. Normally, the monitor is installed in your local hospital outpatient department or GP surgery.

      Do I need to do something different because I am being tested?

      No. Because the test is done to determine what your normal daily blood pressure is, it's important to continue your normal routine and do all the things you normally would. However, you should avoid vigorous exercise.

      Do I have to sleep with the monitor on?

      Yes, if your doctor or nurse requests it, it is important that you keep the monitor on at night. This will help us understand what your blood pressure is doing while you are asleep.
